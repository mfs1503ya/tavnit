Mirrors

Hebrew storytellers often built a passage in matching halves around a center. The flood story climbs to one line and walks back down.

Why does the flood turn on Genesis 8:1?

Gordon Wenham argued in 1978 that the flood narrative pairs its elements in reverse order around “God remembered Noah.” Days, the covenant and Noah’s sons appear on both sides. Not every scholar agrees, so Tavnit draws the structure and names the study it comes from.

Questions

Before you begin.

When can I try the app?

It opens in small groups. Save your spot and we will email you when your place is ready.

Why the name Tavnit?

תַּבְנִית (tavnit) is the Hebrew word for “pattern.” It is the word God uses when He shows Moses the design of the tabernacle: “See to it that you make everything according to the pattern shown you on the mountain” (Exodus 25:40). Tavnit shows you the pattern inside every passage.

Is it for my church?

Tavnit is for anyone who reads the Bible. It uses the 66-book Protestant canon, and its answers stay with what the text says instead of taking sides between traditions.

Do I need to read Hebrew or Greek?

No. Everything is in English. The original words are there when you tap them.

Is this a Bible code site?

No. We do not look for hidden messages. We show structures that scholars have found in the text, and we name who proposed them.

Which translation do you use?

The Berean Standard Bible, a modern English translation in the public domain.
